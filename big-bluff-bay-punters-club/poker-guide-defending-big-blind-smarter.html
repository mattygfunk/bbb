Poker Guide: Defending Big Blind Smarter

This guide is built for practical execution at Big Bluff Bay: what to do, when to do it, and what to avoid. Use it alongside Poker Schedule, Poker FAQ, and Poker Rules so your strategy fits real event conditions.

Core framework

For Defending Big Blind Smarter, your goal is repeatable quality. Build a default line first, then adjust only when there is clear evidence (stack depth, position, player tendency, or payout pressure). This prevents emotional over-adjustment and protects long-run EV.

  • Decide your baseline before action starts.
  • Use position and stack depth as primary filters.
  • Only deviate when the reason is measurable.

Practical in-game checklist

  • Before action: What is my baseline decision here?
  • During action: Did new information justify deviation?
  • After action: Was this technically sound or emotionally driven?

Running this 3-step loop in-session is one of the fastest ways to reduce leak frequency.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: autopilot decisions under time pressure. Fix: pre-commit a baseline for common spots.
  • Mistake: forcing marginal aggression to “take control.” Fix: choose pressure spots where blockers/position support it.
  • Mistake: no review loop after sessions. Fix: use Session Review Scorecard weekly.

30-day improvement drill

  1. Play with one clear objective tied to this guide for two sessions per week.
  2. Tag 5 relevant hands each week.
  3. Review those hands with one sentence: “What should be my default next time?”
  4. Keep or modify your default only after evidence across multiple sessions.

This turns abstract theory into stable decision upgrades.
